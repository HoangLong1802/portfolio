Cleaning decisions

  • Duplicates and conflicts: removed 75 duplicate ticket rows and 36 duplicate work-log rows. Quarantined 60 versions belonging to 30 conflicting ticket IDs because no update timestamp identified the authoritative version.
  • Categories and channels: restored the category on 60 rows where the subcategory identified it uniquely; quarantined 60 ambiguous rows. Trimmed whitespace and normalized letter case in 120 channel values.
  • Ticket lifecycle: quarantined invalid timestamp order, missing required fields and invalid references. Individual issue counts may overlap; the table reports unique source rows.
  • CSAT: set 23 out-of-range scores to null while retaining the ticket. A missing survey response is not treated as a score of zero.
  • Handling time: retained long durations where event timestamps were valid. Read the mean alongside the median and percentiles to show long-wait cases.

Preventing double counting

A ticket can have multiple work logs. Aggregate work logs per ticket before joining to keep the 14,774 ticket grain; distinguish final-owner outcomes from the effort of agents who handled the case.

Demand comparisons use all 365 calendar days in the period: 12,240 / 261 = 46.90 tickets per weekday; 2,534 / 104 = 24.37 per weekend day. The ratio of daily averages is 1.92×.

Interpretation limits

This synthetic dataset is for practice. Quarantining records can change case mix and observable capacity. CSAT represents respondents only; ticket snapshots do not reconstruct every status transition. Daily capacity estimates do not determine exact shift gaps. Recommendations require further validation before being applied to real operations.
